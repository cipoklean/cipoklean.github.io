RemitLink

Send money home with a link.

A US → Nigeria remittance app. The sender signs in with a passkey, sends dollars and gets a claim link. The recipient opens it on WhatsApp, signs in with a fingerprint and claims. No wallet, no seed phrase, and the UI never says "crypto".

  • USDC escrow contract that pays only the committed recipient (23 Foundry tests)
  • Gas sponsored by a bounded relayer, so first-time users need nothing
  • Envio HyperIndex powering live activity and metrics

NullHaven

Compliant privacy pools on Stellar.

Deposit into a shielded pool and withdraw anywhere with no on-chain link, while Association Set Providers keep bad actors out at the circuit level.

  • Proofs generated in the browser; secrets never leave the device
  • Encrypted view keys for authorised auditors

Private yield that prices its own privacy.

About

Weekend deadlines.
Production habits.

I'm David Aghazie, known online as cipoklean, building from Nigeria. Most of what I make starts as a hackathon brief and ends as a live, tested product a judge can click through without reading the docs.

I'm drawn to problems where money, trust and privacy meet: sending money home without the fees and forms, letting AI agents pay each other safely, and making on-chain privacy practical rather than theoretical.

I work across the whole stack, smart contracts to frontend to the AI layer, and I care about the unglamorous parts: tests, threat models, demo modes and honest READMEs.

01
Demo or it didn't happen

Every build ships with a live URL or a judge quick-start that runs with no API key.

02
Hide the machinery

Passkeys over seed phrases, claim links over wallet addresses. Users shouldn't need to know it's on-chain.

03
Honest by design

Agents that abstain when they don't know, and READMEs that mark what's mocked.

04
Security isn't a stretch goal

Threat models, bounded relayers, reentrancy guards and tests, even under a 48-hour clock.
